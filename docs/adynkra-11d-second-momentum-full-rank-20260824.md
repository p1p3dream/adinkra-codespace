## Why one modular computation proves characteristic-zero independence

Let \(M\) denote the exact characteristic-zero projected response matrix over
\(\mathbb Q(i)\), with 77 columns. Clear the finitely many denominators that
occur in the matrix. Static construction checks that every required
denominator is nonzero modulo the pinned prime. Because
\(p=1{,}073{,}741{,}783\) is congruent to 3 modulo 4, adjoining a root of
\(i^2+1\) gives \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\). The fixed functional is linear and
commutes with admissible reduction, producing \(M_p\) over that Gaussian
finite-field extension.

The exact elimination found 77 pivots. Hence some \(77\times77\) minor has
determinant nonzero modulo \(p\). The corresponding cleared
characteristic-zero determinant cannot be zero, because zero would reduce to
zero at every admissible prime. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q(i)} M \ge 77.
\]

There are only 77 columns, so

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q(i)} M = 77,
\qquad
\ker_{\mathbb Q(i)} M = \{0\}.
\]

Additional primes are valuable fault checks but are not required for this
rank lower-bound proof. A separate audit decoded all 77 `ADFXGPU3` artifacts,
streamed the complete `25,344 x 77` residue matrix, independently reproduced
the matrix SHA-256, and again obtained rank 77 and nullity zero at the pinned
prime. That replay was an inline, read-only verification performed against
the production artifacts during review, not a committed second verifier. Its
cite-ready result was:

The conclusion is independence of these 77 exact projected response columns,
